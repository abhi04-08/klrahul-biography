Early Life

Rahul was born on 18 April 1992 to K. N. Lokesh and Rajeshwari in Bangalore, Karnataka in a Kannada-speaking family.[3] His father Lokesh, who hails from Kannanur in Magadi, is a professor and former director at the National Institute of Technology Karnataka (NITK)[4][5] in Mangalore. His mother, Rajeshwari, is a professor at Mangalore University.[6] His father was a fan of cricketer Sunil Gavaskar and wanted to name his own son after Gavaskar's son but mistook Rohan Gavaskar's name for Rahul.[7] Rahul grew up in Surathkal, Mangalore, completing his High School at NITK English Medium School and PUC at St. Aloysius College.[8] He started cricket training at the age of 10, and, two years later, started playing matches for both Bangalore United Cricket Club and his club in Mangalore.[9] At age 18, he moved to Bangalore to study at Jain University and pursue his cricket career.[10][11][12] On 23 January 2023, Rahul married his long-time girlfriend, actress Athiya Shetty daughter of actor Sunil Shetty, after dating for more than three years.[13] Shetty gave birth to a girl, on 24 March 2025.[14]

Domestic Career

Rahul made his first-class cricket debut for Karnataka in the 2010–11 season. In the same season, he represented his country at the 2010 ICC Under-19 Cricket World Cup, scoring 143 runs in the competition.[15] He made his debut in the Indian Premier League in 2013, for Royal Challengers Bangalore.[16] During the 2013–14 domestic season he scored 1,033 first-class runs, the second highest scorer that season. Playing for South Zone in the final of the 2014–15 Duleep Trophy against Central Zone, Rahul scored 185 off 233 balls in the first innings and 130 off 152 in the second. He was named the player of the match and selected to the Indian Test squad for the Australian tour followed. Returning home after the Test series, Rahul became Karnataka's first triple-centurion, scoring 337 against Uttar Pradesh.[17] He went on to score 188 in the 2014–15 Ranji Trophy final against Tamil Nadu and finished the season with an average of 93.11 in the nine matches he played. In January 2026, Rahul returned to represent Karnataka in the Vijay Hazare Trophy after a six-year gap, having last appeared in the tournament in 2019.[18]

International Career

KL Rahul made his Test debut in 2014, scoring a maiden international century soon after. He faced setbacks due to injuries and illness, including dengue and training injuries, but made notable comebacks with strong performances, such as his career-best 199 runs in a Test match in 2017. Rahul was the first Indian cricketer to score a century on ODI debut in 2016 and quickly became the fastest batsman to score centuries in all three formats. He encountered a suspension in 2019 following controversial comments but returned to play in the 2019 Cricket World Cup, where he was India's third-highest run scorer. Although dropped from Test squads due to form issues, he remained a key player in limited-overs formats, earning man of the series awards and vice-captaincy roles. Rahul demonstrated consistent performance in T20I and ODI series against teams like West Indies, New Zealand, and Australia, scoring multiple centuries and match-winning innings. In 2021, he excelled in England Test series with solid batting techniques and was India's second-highest run scorer on that tour. Overall, Rahul's career is marked by resilience, adaptability across formats, and significant contributions to Indian cricket.

IPL Career

KL Rahul began his IPL career with Royal Challengers Bangalore in 2013 and later played for Sunrisers Hyderabad in 2014 before returning to RCB in 2016, where he scored 397 runs and earned a place in the IPL XI. He missed the 2017 season due to injury but made a strong comeback in 2018 with Kings XI Punjab, scoring 659 runs and hitting the fastest IPL fifty (14 balls). In 2019, he became vice-captain, scored 593 runs, and registered his first IPL century. He was appointed captain in 2020 and had a record-breaking season with 670 runs, winning the Orange Cap and setting a high score of 132*. He continued as captain in 2021, scoring 626 runs. Ahead of 2022, he joined Lucknow Super Giants as captain for ₹17 crore and scored two centuries, leading the team to playoffs. In 2023, he was ruled out mid-season due to injury. In 2024, he scored 520 runs but failed to take his team to playoffs and faced criticism after a heavy defeat. Subsequently, he was released before the 2025 auction and was bought by Delhi Capitals for ₹14 crore, marking a new phase in his IPL career.
